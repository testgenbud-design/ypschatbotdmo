## How admission works at Yashoda

Here is how admission works at Yashoda Technical Campus, Satara. Most seats are filled through the Centralized Admission Process (CAP) conducted by the Maharashtra State Common Entrance Test Cell. The complete CAP schedule, rounds and merit lists are published on the CET Cell website (cetcell.mahacet.org) and on dtemaharashtra.gov.in. The campus admission office updates its own admission notices on yes.edu.in during the admission season.

The entrance route depends on the programme:

- B.Tech and M.Tech: through CAP after MHT-CET (and applicable national entrance scores as per State CET Cell rules for the year).
- MBA and MCA: through CAP after the MAH-MBA/MMS-CET and MAH-MCA-CET respectively.
- B.Pharmacy, Pharm D and D.Pharmacy: as per the CET Cell and PCI norms published for the year.
- Diploma in Engineering (Polytechnic): after SSC through the centralised admission process for first-year diploma.

## Institute-level and vacant seats at Yashoda

After the CAP rounds, institute-level seats and seats remaining vacant after CAP are filled by the campus admission office at Yashoda Technical Campus, Satara, as per the rules published for that admission year. Parents and students can contact the admission office directly about institute-level admission in any faculty — Engineering, Polytechnic, Pharmacy, MBA or MCA. The office can tell you which branches still have vacant seats, what the merit position needs to look like, and how the institute-level round is scheduled. Many Satara-district families take this route after the CAP rounds close, so it is worth calling the office early rather than waiting.

## Eligibility for each programme at Yashoda (पात्रता)

How does eligibility (पात्रता, eligibility criteria) work at Yashoda? The affiliating body and the Maharashtra State CET Cell decide the exact criteria for every admission year, and cut-offs change from year to year — so the office always confirms the numbers rather than quoting old ones. As a general guide:

- B.Tech first year: HSC (12th) Science with Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics, through the CET Cell CAP process
- B.Tech direct second year: a recognised engineering diploma in a relevant branch
- B.Pharmacy and Pharm D: HSC Science as per PCI and CET Cell norms
- D.Pharmacy and Polytechnic diploma: SSC (10th) as per the centralised process
- M.Tech: a relevant B.E./B.Tech degree; M.Pharmacy: B.Pharmacy; MBA: any recognised graduation through MAH-MBA/MMS-CET; MCA: graduation with the required mathematics/computer background through MAH-MCA-CET

Category, home-university and minority candidature types follow the rules published by the CET Cell for the year. The admission office at Yashoda checks your exact eligibility, category and documents before you apply, so the safest next step is a phone call or a campus visit with your marksheets.

## Documents to keep ready for admission at Yashoda

Keep the originals and a few photocopies ready before CAP reporting or institute-level admission. Commonly required documents are:

- SSC (10th) marksheet and HSC (12th) marksheet, or diploma marksheets for direct second-year and post-graduate entry
- Entrance exam scorecard (MHT-CET, MAH-MBA-CET, MAH-MCA-CET or as applicable)
- School or college leaving certificate / transfer certificate
- Domicile certificate and nationality proof as per state rules
- Caste certificate and caste validity certificate, where applicable
- Non-creamy layer certificate, where applicable
- Income certificate for scholarship, EBC or TFWS benefits, where applicable
- Aadhaar card, passport-size photographs, and gap affidavit if there is a study gap

The admission office at Yashoda confirms the final list for your programme and category.

## After admission

Once a seat is allotted and confirmed, students complete enrolment at the campus at Wadhe, Satara. Fee payment can be made online through the institute's payment portal, and hostel admission is done separately through the campus hostel office. Orientation and induction programmes (such as the Arambh induction programme) welcome new students at the start of the academic year.
